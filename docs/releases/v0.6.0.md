**FreeGaz is now CrankHog.** Same app, same rides, new name — and a hog.

## The new name

Everything you see has changed: the wordmark, the app icon, the Mac app itself
(`CrankHog.app`), the phone app, and the site, now at
[crankhog.app](https://crankhog.app).

**Your rides are safe and you don't have to do anything.** The first time 0.6.0
starts it moves `~/Library/Application Support/FreeGaz` to `…/CrankHog` and
`~/Documents/FreeGaz/Rides` to `~/Documents/CrankHog/Rides`, and carries on.
Under the hood the database keeps its old name on purpose, so every ride,
workout, route and setting comes across untouched.

If you had both versions installed at once, CrankHog leaves the old FreeGaz
folder exactly where it is rather than trying to merge the two, and uses the new
one. Nothing is deleted either way.

## If you ride the phone app from a home screen

The web app moved with the name, from `raytfitzgerald.github.io/FreeGaz` to
[raytfitzgerald.github.io/CrankHog](https://raytfitzgerald.github.io/CrankHog/).

**This is the one thing you have to do by hand.** If you added the old address
to your home screen, that icon points at the old path: open the new one and add
it again, then delete the old icon. Your rides are not affected — they are
stored against the site, not the path — but the old icon will not find the app.

Nothing to do if you use the Mac app, the App Store app, or just a browser tab.

## Meet Sir Loin

There is a hog now. He is the mascot, he is faster than you, and he has never
been FTP tested.

- **He laps the velodrome on Home,** up on the stayers' line, every week.
- **On a week you haven't ridden, he has the whole track to himself,** and your
  coach has opinions about that.
- **He races you the whole way down a route.** His pace wanders either side of
  your FTP, so the lead changes hands: get clear and he chases, and he is
  quicker when he is cross. He cannot get more than 450 m up the road, because
  past that he stops and waits where you can see him. The gap reads out over
  the road ahead, and he rides into the ride-along whenever you close on him.
- **He turns up every minute or two, doing something about the road.** He grinds
  up a wall in the big ring, tucks down a descent and vanishes, pulls a wheelie
  for no reason, sits on your wheel and then gets bored. Push over your FTP and
  he applauds from the verge. Soft pedal and he has a lie down, or holds up a
  sign, or stops for a sandwich. Occasionally he rides into a hedge on purpose.
- **He trots along the verge behind the ride-along,** on foot, getting overtaken.
  It is the only time you beat him.
- **He is on the app icon,** on the top straight.

With Reduce Motion turned on he stops moving: whatever he is doing still gets
its line, as a quiet status message, and he doesn't trot through the background
at all.

## Miles by default

New installs now start in miles, feet and pounds. **If you already use
CrankHog, nothing changes** — whatever you had stays, including if you never
opened the Units setting at all. Change it any time in **Settings → Units**,
and speed and weight can still each differ from the rest (miles and kilograms,
if that's you).

## Somewhere to ride

The ride-along now rides through country instead of across a blank panel: hills
on the horizon, trees and telegraph poles, a barn, birds, and a field of hogs
who are not interested in your ride. Each layer drifts at its own distance, so
the road has depth, and it all speeds up and slows down with you.

A distance board goes past every kilometre — or mile, if that's your setting —
counting the ride's own distance.

Reduce Motion holds the lot still.

## CrankHog on your iPhone

There is a real iPhone app now, on the App Store, and it drives the trainer.
No browser can speak Bluetooth on iOS, so this one is a native shell around the
same app, talking to your trainer and strap over CoreBluetooth directly.
Remembered devices reconnect by themselves.

Rides record and export the same way they do on the Mac. FIT files land in
**Files → On My iPhone → CrankHog**, backups and workouts go out through the
share sheet, and the screen stays awake while you ride.

It is iPhone-only for this first release, and it ships the eight character
coaches rather than all ten: App Review takes a dim view of caricatures built
from photographs of real politicians, so The Donald and Bibi stay on the Mac
and the web. If you restore a backup that was set to one of them, the app quietly
falls back to a coach it has.

## Try it without a trainer

**Devices → Try demo mode** connects a simulated trainer and heart-rate strap,
so you can ride the whole app with nothing clipped in — useful on a train, and
useful for anyone who wants a look before buying hardware. It is also on the
ride screen, next to Connect devices. Rides recorded in demo mode are flagged
simulated, exactly like the Mac's simulator, so they never upload and never
count towards your fitness.

It stays on until you turn it off from the same card.

## Journeys roll downhill again

With journeys on, a missing power reading freewheels the way a real road does:
you roll to a stop on the flat and pick up speed on a descent, instead of
stopping dead. Pausing the ride still holds the dot still and reads zero.

## Everything else

No changes to recording, workouts, the builder, FTP tests, FIT export, Strava
or intervals.icu.
